EES (Entry/Exit System)

The EES is a new EU digital system for recording entries, exits, and refusals of entry for non-EU citizens arriving for a short stay. It replaces manual passport stamps with a digital record. The system also captures document details, a facial photo, and fingerprints.

Who does it apply to

The EES applies to non-EU citizens traveling on a short stay—that is, up to 90 days within any 180-day period. This applies to both visa-free travelers and those traveling on a Schengen short-stay C visa. In other words, if a person has a standard Schengen tourist/business visa C, the EES applies to them.

Who is not affected by the EES

As a rule, the system does not apply to holders of a long-stay visa D and a residence permit. In other words, if a person has a national long-stay visa or a residence permit, this is a different regime, and the EES for short-term entry does not usually apply to them.

Important

For the EES system, there is no need to fill out a form in advance; there is no fee, and registration takes place at the border, port, or airport. Upon your first entry, an electronic record is created, which remains valid for 3 years. Entry is registered at the first airport in the Schengen Area, and exit is registered at the last airport in the Schengen Area.

ETIAS

ETIAS is a new pre-entry authorization system for the EU intended for citizens of visa-exempt countries who plan to enter 30 European countries for a short-term stay. It is not a visa and does not replace a visa in cases where one is required.

Who does it apply to

ETIAS applies to travelers from visa-exempt countries and territories who are traveling to participating European countries for a short-term stay of up to 90 days within any 180-day period. It is intended for purposes such as tourism, business trips, visiting relatives, short-term study, medical treatment, or transit.

Who is not affected by ETIAS

ETIAS does not apply to travelers who require a Schengen visa, as they must continue to follow the standard visa procedure. In other words, if a person is traveling with a short-stay Schengen C visa or holds another visa status, ETIAS does not apply to them.

Important

The ETIAS system has not yet been implemented, and travelers are not required to take any action at this time. ETIAS is an online authorization obtained prior to travel, unlike the EES, which is a border registration system.
